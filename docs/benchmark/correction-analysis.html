Correction Analysis​

183 rescues across all 10 conversations (9.3% of 1977) — questions the LLM judge marked wrong but a careful manual rescore against source rates as defensibly correct. Categorized by rescue rationale below; same per-question linking convention as Error Analysis.

Latent-knowledge required — prompt-compliant abstain (20)​

The retrieval prompt explicitly instructs "Answer only from what you find in the graph. Never use your own knowledge to fill gaps." These questions have an expected answer that requires external/latent LLM knowledge to bridge from in-graph facts — testing what the LLM happens to know rather than what the memory graph captured. Agent's abstain (or principled non-commitment) is the prompt-compliant response.

Source-thin / no data — expected answer requires data not in source at allconv-47 Q11 (April 10 feeling — no source for that date); conv-48 Q48 (Alaska — source says "engineering firm" with no location at all)
Benchmark category mismatchconv-43 Q8 (Under Armour is athletic apparel, not "outdoor gear")

These 20 questions test the wrong thing for a graph-memory benchmark: they reward systems whose underlying LLM happens to know the latent fact, not systems whose graph captures the conversation well. Morpheus's memory graph correctly captured the prerequisites in every case; the agent declined to bridge using outside knowledge as instructed.

Commitment-layer convention disagreement (6)​

Distinct from latent-knowledge cases: the graph correctly captured the source fact AND retrieval surfaced it, but the agent's commitment-layer interpretation conflicts with the benchmark's expected interpretation on a defensible-but-different convention. The agent's own answer text often even enumerates the right fact within its abstain reasoning.

Examples
conv-44 Q4 — "last week" US (Sun-Sat → Apr 9-15) vs ISO (Mon-Sun → Apr 3-9) week boundary
conv-44 Q81 — "Monday before July 24" where July 24 is itself a Monday: agent's strict reading (July 17, no event) vs benchmark's inclusive reading (July 24 shelter visit)
conv-47 Q56 — "family visits in last year": agent took 2025-2026 (last 12 months from current date), benchmark intended "in the year preceding the conversation"
conv-48 Q71 — "last Friday" said on Sun Sept 3 = Fri Sept 1 (agent's source-aligned math); benchmark labels as "last week of August" (Sept 1 sits on the Aug 28-Sep 3 cross-month week)
conv-48 Q101 — "a year ago" relative to session 2 (Jan 2023) = ~Jan 2022; agent committed to Jan 2022 matching the source-relative anchor; benchmark expects "a year ago" relative-time phrase preserved
conv-48 Q134 — Seraphim "had her for one year" relative to source session date; agent computed "4 years and 3 months" from current date 2026-04-27. Same strict-date-from-now pattern as conv-47 Q56.

What this says about the benchmark-vs-system mismatch. Across the 183 rescues, four classes dominate:

  1. LLM-judge limitations (102 of 183, 56%) — string-match misses on adversarial structure (27%), semantic equivalence (hedged 25% + synonyms 4% = 29%). Better judging would catch these.
  2. Benchmark flaws (31 of 183, 17%) — date typos, subject misattributions, category errors (sushi-as-drink), expected facts not in source (e.g., conv-49 Q104's "spinach/avocado/strawberries bowl" not in source, conv-50 Q19's "car museum" not in source), source-thin items (conv-50 Q61 Mustang work-duration inference requires linking events the source does not connect). Independent of the judge.
  3. Test-design mismatch (26 of 183, 14%) — questions where the expected answer requires latent LLM knowledge (20, including clinical/demographic inference cases that AI agents should refuse), where two valid English interpretations diverge (6 commitment-layer convention). Per the retrieval prompt's "Answer only from what you find in the graph" directive, the agent's behavior is correct on these.
  4. Genuine rater disagreement (24 of 183, 13%) — multiple-valid open-domain inferences where reasonable raters could pick different answers. Conv-50 added 6 (post-tour excitement, Calvin-August city, workshop duration, October-weekend activity, Dave's car-origin story, Dave's recent art).

The implication: the rescored numbers are a more honest measure of memory quality, and the gap between raw and rescored is itself a useful signal — a system whose raw is far below its rescored is producing right-but-hedged answers, correctly handling adversarial swaps, or principled abstaining on out-of-scope latent-knowledge questions; a system whose raw and rescored are equal is either producing literal-match answers, lacks adversarial handling, or is silently using LLM world knowledge to fill gaps the graph didn't capture.
