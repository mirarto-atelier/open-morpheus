Inference Cost

Morpheus uses Opus 4.7 with effort=max for both ingest (graph generation) and retrieval (answering benchmark questions). This page measures token usage on both sides — what does it cost to build the graph, and what does it cost to query it?

Overall headline numbers:

PhaseWall budgetTotal tokensTotal cost
Ingest (272 sessions across 10 conversations)~55 hours3.39 B tokens~$16,140
Retrieval (all 1,977 questions, measured)~12.8 hours224 M tokens~$1,097

The graph is ~15× more expensive to build than to query. Once built, retrieval is cheap per question but the long tail and large suite size make total retrieval cost meaningful — about 6.4% of total benchmark cost.

Multi-hop is the cheapest category despite its name. Conventional wisdom would expect multi-hop to dominate cost (more reasoning to chain facts), but the data shows the opposite — multi-hop is even cheaper than temporal. Why? The retrieval prompt directs multi-hop questions to specific entities, and the cross-branch involves / instance_of links surface the chain in a single neighborhood expansion. The agent commits in ~4 turns. Compare with open-domain at $1.083 (2.7× more expensive than multi-hop) — open-domain triggers the agent to chase inference signals across multiple subgraphs before committing, with mean 8.8 turns and a 275K stdev (the highest variance of any category, because some open-domain questions have direct evidence and resolve quickly while others require sweeping the graph).

Adversarial is the second-most expensive at $0.762. The retrieval prompt explicitly instructs the agent to verify subject before answering — that subject-verification step plus the actual content sweep produces the high mean turns (7.2). The yield is worth it: 99.3% rescored adversarial accuracy.

Distribution histogram​

The distribution clusters tightly in the 30-100K range with a long tail extending into hundreds of thousands. Mean (113K) is pulled significantly above median (70K) by the tail. The 99th percentile (577K) is 8× the median.

Per-category overlay shows the distinct shapes: multi-hop / temporal pile near the low end (~50-80K mean); adversarial spreads further right (subject-verification overhead); open-domain has the heaviest right tail (deep inference chains).

Per-session ingest costs are ~3 orders of magnitude larger than per-question retrieval costs. This reflects the asymmetry: ingest does deep extraction with one-fact-per-leaf granularity and procedural-pitfall auto-spawning across the full session content, while retrieval is targeted graph traversal driven by a single question.

Hypothesis test: does cost plateau as the graph saturates?​

The hypothesis going in: early sessions should be cheaper (graph is empty, agent has less existing context to consider), and later sessions should plateau.

The slope is statistically indistinguishable from zero (t = 0.14, far below the t > 2 threshold for 95% significance). The 95% confidence interval on the slope is [−3.5, +4.0] K tokens/turn per session — the data is consistent with a perfectly flat plateau, OR with a tiny drift in either direction. The added 30 conv-50 sessions tightened the confidence interval and pushed the t-statistic even closer to zero.

Findings — hypothesis confirmed​

  1. Cold-start is dramatic. Session 1 averages 159 K tokens / turn (n=10) vs sessions 2+ averaging 609 K tokens / turn. A ~4× cold-start gap — with an empty graph, the agent has nothing to cross-reference and extracts each fact independently.

  2. Post-cold-start, the cost per turn is genuinely flat. Bucketed averages stay in the 438-680 K range with no monotonic trend:

    Session rangenMean K tokens / turn
    1 (cold start)10159
    2-540663
    6-1050664
    11-1550438
    16-2048619
    21-2540678
    26-3234619

    The variance across session positions (438-678) is smaller than the within-position variance (stdev ~165-285) at any given session number. There's no signal in the position dimension — only noise from session-level content differences.

  3. Cache_read drives the plateau. Cache_read dominates ingest tokens (~85%). As the graph grows, neighborhood-expansion queries return larger context — but the agent's per-turn working set is bounded by what it actually needs to retrieve to extract the current session, not by graph total size. Graph growth is sublinear in session count once the major entities are established, so cache_read per turn stabilizes at the agent's typical query depth.

The takeaway​

The hypothesis is strongly confirmed: post-cold-start, ingest cost per turn is flat at ~605 K, with no statistically detectable trend over 30+ sessions. The cold-start gap is the single largest factor — once the agent has any graph to reference, per-turn cost stabilizes at ~4× the cold-start rate and stays there.

Practical consequence: ingest cost per conversation scales as (one cold-start session) + (linear in total turn count). For a 30-session conversation with ~19 turns/session like Calvin & Dave, that's ~6 M (cold start) + ~29 × 19 × 605 K ≈ ~340 M tokens — within 8% of the observed 315 M total.

The entire 10-conversation LOCOMO suite — graph generation + answering all 1,977 benchmark questions — costs roughly the price of a mid-tier laptop. Per question, that's $8.72 fully-loaded (ingest + retrieval), or $0.555 marginal if the graph already exists.

Caveats and notes​

  • Cost calculations use Opus 4 standard-tier prices: input $15/MTok, cache write 5m $18.75/MTok, cache read $1.50/MTok, output $75/MTok.
  • Retrieval costs reported here are from the original benchmark runs mined from the ~/.claude/projects/ archive. These are not re-runs and reflect actual benchmark execution cost.
  • Ingest used effort=max Opus 4.7 with one-pass extraction. A multi-pass refinement run would substantially increase ingest cost but might improve cross-concept linking and reduce the list-completeness misses observed in the Error Analysis.
  • The 5-min ephemeral cache window matters — running questions in tight succession amortizes cache creation across more turns. The original benchmark ran sequentially per conversation, so the cache hit rate within a conversation is high but new conversations pay full cache-create cost on the first few questions.
